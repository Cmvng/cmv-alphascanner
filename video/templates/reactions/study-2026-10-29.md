# Your reactions: a creative study (29 Oct 2026)

**The idea in one line:** your own face is the meme. Nine silent reactions become a library of stickers. One of them
answers the moment whenever a line is funny, shocking, painful or doubtful. It works in a screen recording, a
motion-graphics explainer, or a video where you're already on camera, and it always looks the same, so people know it's you.

**Why it works:**
- A silent face takes its meaning from the moment just before it. This is the Kuleshov effect, which a 2016 study
  confirmed with a weak to moderate effect [1]. Your clips have no words, so they fit any line.
- Faces hold attention. In 6.9 million edX viewing sessions, slides mixed with the teacher's face were more engaging
  than slides alone [2]. TikTok's own guidance says to feature people and use stickers to keep viewers watching [3].
- **It's your lane.** Crypto already reacts to the chart with memes: Pink Wojak, the crying face on every crash, dates
  from 2018 [4]. Putting your own real face in that role is new, and so is a "called it / missed it" stamp with your
  face. I found no precedent for either.
- **It's yours to use.** You never need famous meme photos again, which we can't use anyway.

## 1. The library (`library-sheet.jpg`)

| | Name | The feeling | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Grin** | proud, told you so | a call that came off, a pump, a cheap fee |
| 2 | **Jaw Drop** | amazed, no way | a big number, a launch, a stunning goal |
| 3 | **Wait, What?** | disbelief, confused | a number that sounds wrong, a self-own |
| 4 | **Yikes** | cringe, that'll hurt | high fees, a bad entry, awful away form |
| 5 | **It's Over** | disaster, hands on head | a dump, a 90th-minute goal against |
| 6 | **LOL** | hilarious | an absurd claim, a silly coin name, an own goal |
| 7 | **Side-Eye** | not convinced, sure buddy | "Bitcoin might go up today", a promise that sounds too good |
| 8 | **The Sob** | gutted, a small loss | a missed call, "Chelsea might lose today" |
| 9 | **The Wail** | devastated, then angry | the bottom of a crash, a heavy defeat |

How they're made:
- Each clip is cut out of the room with a video-matting model, so the hair and beard edges hold steady from frame to frame.
- Each is trimmed to its strongest 3–4 seconds and given a white die-cut border.
- They **move**: every sticker plays your real clip, never a still.
- For full-screen cutaways, the original clip with your room is used.

**More angles for later:**
- **Mood meter:** your face follows a number, such as the Crypto Fear & Greed Index or a team's win chance.
- **The reply:** a quoted claim or post on screen, then your face answers it.
- **Your sticker pack:** release the nine as Telegram stickers or Twitch emotes, so fans spread your face for you [5][6].

## 3. The rules (what keeps it premium, not a gimmick)

1. **One locked style.** The same white die-cut border, one soft shadow, a small tilt and one pop (in 0.2 s with a small
   overshoot, out in 0.12 s). The sameness is the signature. Changing it each time is what makes it look like a gimmick.
2. **React, don't announce.** Land on the first frame after the word. Side-Eye and Wait, What? can come a beat early,
   while the doubtful line is still being said. Comedy editors put the difference between funny and not funny at two
   frames [7].
3. **Short.** A corner sticker stays 0.8–1.6 s. An interrupt stays under 1 s, and never more than once per video.
4. **Not too often.** Two to four per minute, never two within about 6 seconds. Save It's Over and The Wail for the
   climax.
5. **Free space only:**
   - never on your face or the thing being explained;
   - never on the captions (in our layout they start about 190 px above the bottom);
   - never under the platform buttons on the right;
   - TikTok's own interface covers about 320 px at the bottom and 120–164 px on the right [8].
6. **Real over exaggerated.** In vidIQ's study of breakout thumbnails, the winners showed "a genuine expression that
   matched the video", not the biggest face [9]. Use each face for what it really means.
7. **One per meaning.** A face that means "called it" always means that, so viewers learn your vocabulary.

## 4. Sound: your call

The demos have **no sound effect** on the stickers, because you've said no whooshes. Research says sound matters on
TikTok: 88% of users call it essential [10]. A single signature sound, very soft and the same every time, could become
part of the brand. **Do you want one?** If yes, I'll send you three 5-second options to choose from.

## 5. What to record next (same grey T-shirt, same wall and light as these: that's what makes them a set)

Record each one for 4–5 seconds: start neutral for half a second, hold the face for 2–3 seconds, then return to
neutral.
- **Pointing down at the screen**, a very useful "look at this".
- **Thinking**, finger on the temple.
- **Facepalm.**
- **Slow clap.**
- **Eye roll.**
- **Nodding yes** and **shaking no**.
- **Mind blown**, hands opening from the head.
- **Chef's kiss.**
- **Shrug.**
- **Rubbing fingers**, for money.
- **Shush.**
- **A neutral blink**, so the mood bubble has a resting face.

## 6. Honest limits of the research

- I couldn't verify how often, for how long or where specific creators (Coffeezilla, Graham Stephan, Caleb Hammer and
  others) use reaction inserts. That would need a frame-by-frame study of their videos.
- The timing and frequency rules above are editing practice and my own synthesis, not measured results.
