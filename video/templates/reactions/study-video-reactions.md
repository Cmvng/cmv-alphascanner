# Your video reactions: the creative study, reworked (29 Oct 2026)

**The idea:** your nine clips are reaction videos: you, on camera in your room, reacting with no words. When a line in
one of your videos is funny, shocking, painful or doubtful, one of them plays as the reaction to it. It plays as a
video, at real speed and with your room, big enough to watch you react. It works:
- in a screen recording, where you aren't on camera;
- in an explainer;
- in a video where you're already talking to camera.

The video's voice keeps going under it, then the video carries on.

**What I got wrong the first time:** I cut your face out, put a white sticker border round it, made it small and
showed it for about a second. That turned your reaction videos into stickers. Now every reaction plays as the video
it is: the room, your movement, the whole beat.

## What I checked in your clips
- **Each clip holds one reaction for 5 to 8 seconds**: a held face, not a quick flash.
  - Some have a shape: It's Over starts with your hands flying onto your head; The Wail builds to the big open
    mouth at 1 s, then cries; Side-Eye looks away twice and comes back.
  - The reactions use these moves. The hook uses the hands landing, and Side-Eye uses the look away and the look
    back.
- **The sound is room tone only** (about −43 dB, no laugh or breath you can hear). So your video's own voice and
  music carry every reaction. Nothing is added.

## Why it works (sources at the end)
- **The reaction shot is film grammar.** An editor cuts from what happens to the face of someone watching. The face
  carries the feeling and sets the comic timing [1]. What comes just before a face decides what it means: the
  Kuleshov effect, confirmed in a 2016 study [2]. Your silent clips take their meaning from your line, so one clip
  fits many lines.
- **Every platform has a built-in reaction layout, so viewers read it instantly:**
  - TikTok Duet: side by side, top and bottom, and "React", a streamer-style picture-in-picture [3][4];
  - TikTok Stitch, Green Screen and Green Screen Video [5][6];
  - YouTube Shorts Remix: Green Screen and side-by-side Collab [7];
  - Instagram Remix: split screen, green screen and picture-in-picture [8].

  Our formats are those same layouts.
- **Wordless reactions can be a whole brand.** Khaby Lame became the most-followed person on TikTok without saying a
  word: his face reacts, mostly in duets and stitches [9][10].
- **Faces at the right moments work better than a face all the time.**
  - In 12,468 learners, showing the face only at times made people feel more connected [11].
  - In 6.9 million edX sessions, videos that cut between the face and the slides held attention better than
    slides alone [12].

  So reactions come at moments, not as a face cam that's always on.

**Two moves on top of the formats:**
- **The react-first hook.** The video opens on your reaction before we see the reason. Held Too Long now starts
  with It's Over, your hands flying onto your head, while you say "Since the 26th of September…".
  - TikTok's own advice: hook in the first 6 seconds with emotion and a person on screen [13].
  - One guide's reasoning: "the viewer wants to know what caused the reaction" [14] (no data behind it).
- **The payoff.** The hook's reaction comes back, in full, at the moment it was about. At 3:20 It's Over returns in
  a Duet under "it looks like it's a stop loss". The opening was a promise, and this pays it off.

**Your two example lines:**
- **"Bitcoin might go up today" → Side-Eye.** In Held Too Long you say "I expect Bitcoin to go to 90,000", and your

## The rules (what keeps it premium)
1. **One look, every time.** Same frame, ring and pop, same seam on the Duet, same push-in on the Cutaway. The sameness
   is what people will recognise as yours. Vary the format, never the look.
2. **Land on the word, then hold until it peaks.**
   - Each reaction starts on the word it answers ("flags", "rally", "loss").
   - Film editors: a listener reacts before the speaker finishes, so cut to the reaction a few frames in [15].
   - Hold "until the reaction peaks and starts to fade, then cut" [16].
3. **Long enough to watch, and big** (your note: shorter or smaller is "not visibly evident… too fast"):
   - Reaction Cam: about 3 s, about half the frame wide;
   - Duet: 3–6 s;
   - Cutaway: about 2 s;
   - Freeze: about 2 s.
4. **Not too often.** Two or three a minute, with the strongest saved for the climax. Held Too Long has 10 in 5:14;
   Primus has 5 in 1:40.
5. **Free space only.**
   - Never on the thing being explained.
   - Never on the platform buttons. TikTok covers about the bottom 320 px and the right 120–164 px.
   - Never under a caption: a window ends above the caption box.
6. **Captions stay on top.** When a reaction covers the frame, the video's own captions are lifted out of its frames
   and drawn over the reaction: the same words, the same place, the same style. Sound-off viewers never lose a line.
7. **One meaning per face.**
   - Side-Eye always means "sure, buddy", even when it's at yourself; the Grin always means it came off.
   - Using a face twice with the same meaning is a callback. Held Too Long has two Side-Eyes, both at your own
     bullish target.
8. **Inside videos with your voice.** YouTube doesn't pay for "mostly non-verbal reactions… without added voice
   commentary" [17]. A video made only of reactions wouldn't earn on YouTube; your reactions inside your own
   commentary are fine.

- **The always-on reaction cam.** For long screen recordings: a small window of you watching, which changes to a

## What to record next (same grey T-shirt, wall and light, so the set matches)
4–6 s each, starting neutral:
- **Pointing down at the screen** ("look at this"): for From Behind and the Duet.
- **Leaning into the camera**, eyes wide: zoom energy without a zoom.
- **Neutral, watching** (30–60 s, small nods, a blink): for the always-on reaction cam.
- **Facepalm**, **slow clap**, **eye roll**, **nodding yes**, **shaking head no**, **mind blown**, **shrug**.

## Sound
Nothing is added to the reactions: no whoosh, no sound effect. Your video's voice and music carry them. If you ever
want one signature sound for the reactions, say so and I'll send you three short options to choose from.

## Honest limits
- The research didn't find measured data on Duets, Stitches or reaction inserts and retention; the uplift figures
  online cite no source.
- Facecam sizes and split ratios have no standard. Ours are sized for a phone: the window is about 45% of the frame
  width so your face reads.
- The timing rules are editing practice, not measurements.
