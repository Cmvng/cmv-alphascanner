PICKING A REACTION THAT FITS THE LINE  (7 Nov 2026; goes with context_map.json and PLAYBOOK.md)

The face must match what the line MEANS TO THE VIEWER, at the SIZE we show it.
Judge each clip by how it reads at sticker size (see "reads_at_sticker_size" in context_map.json), not by its name.

STEP 0. SET AND BUDGET (once per video)
  - BOTH sets are one library (owner, 7 Nov: "I have two separate reaction videos... why you're not mixing them"):
      GREY T-shirt  = grin, jawdrop, waitwhat, yikes, itsover, lol, sideeye, sob, wail (+ their _full arcs) + Bagel recording
      SHIRTLESS     = stare, wince, noway, ugh, shook, crackup, nervous, nah, hmm + Cournot recording
    Pick the best-fitting clip from either set. Keep one set within a single appearance (no grey->shirtless swap in place).
  - Budget (owner, 7 Nov: "clean and simple... too many reactions... looks disjointed"): AT MOST 3 per video,
    1-2 in the body plus the sign-off; at least 10 s apart; at most ONE intensity-3 face per video.
    No listening sticker parked on screen through scenes: come in just before the moment, react, leave.
  - First list every candidate line in the script, then rank them. The biggest moment gets the strongest face; the rest
    get their honest size or nothing.

STEP 1. WHAT DOES THE LINE MEAN TO THE VIEWER?  Read the whole sentence and the next one, never just the keyword.
  Put it in exactly one class:
    GOOD          a win, a gain, a fair or cheap thing, a feature that protects you
    BAD           a loss, a cost, someone taking more than they should
    RISKY         something that could go wrong but hasn't (a rule, leverage, access that continues)
    SUSPICIOUS    a bold claim, a promise, "trust me", a prediction: a claim to doubt
    ABSURD        silly, ironic, a self-own, a joke
    SURPRISING    information the viewer could not predict (note whether it is good or bad)
    TEACHING      a definition, how it works, the mechanics of an example, a number being read out
    REJECTION     "not this", "you don't need X", "don't do this"
    PAYOFF        the solution lands, the verdict, the sign-off ("anyways")
  Then check three things:
    a. Who is it happening to?  The viewer or narrator (empathy), a third party or bad actor (judgement), or nobody
       (a hypothetical: teaching).
    b. Does the script soften it?  "Let's say", "just an example", "my example", "paper money", "testnet" -> the line is
       TEACHING with a flavour, and drops one intensity level.
    c. Is it phrased against its meaning?  "Not the 47 trades, the $8,200 or your login" is a GOOD payoff phrased as a
       REJECTION; "Team loses, $0" inside a worked example is TEACHING, not a loss; "at 20% it closes everything" is a
       RISKY rule, not an event.
  Football: our side scores = GOOD; a goal against = BAD; a pundit's or tipster's certainty = SUSPICIOUS; a stat read
  out = TEACHING unless it is the point of the segment.

STEP 2. HOW BIG IS IT?
    1 minor   a detail, one number among several, one step of a mechanism, an example's branch
    2 major   the point of a section: the problem stated, the catch, the solution
    3 hero    the single biggest moment of the video, where the script leaves a 0.4-1 s beat
  - Unsure -> go one lower.
  - Climax bonus: the ONE biggest beat of the video may go one level above its size (max 3), once.
  - Softened lines (Step 1b) drop one level. A hypothetical never reaches 3 unless it is that one climax AND it is the
    video's warning to the viewer.
  - Flash rule: an intensity-2 face shown for <=1.2 s and returned to listening counts as 1. Intensity-3 faces cannot be
    flashed down: they read big however short.

STEP 3. IS THERE A VISIBLE STIMULUS AT THAT INSTANT?
  The number, card, stamp, score or bar must be on screen, near the sticker, when the face changes.
  - No stimulus -> cap at 1 (listening face, a look, a lean) or skip. "You can't fake intensity."
  - The stimulus must also settle the valence for faces that don't (Jaw Drop: green +900% vs red candle).

STEP 4. TONE AND ARC
  - Calm explainer: routine beats cap at 2; the climax may be 3. Hype or match reaction: 3 allowed on the biggest moment.
  - The problem escalates: each BAD/RISKY beat is >= the one before (never hard -> soft while the script says it gets
    worse). TEACHING beats never escalate.
  - The payoff is the only big positive face; the end card is a callback of an earlier face WITH THE SAME MEANING.
  - One face, one meaning per video. Spacing: >=5 s between reactions, >=2 s inside a deliberate chain.

STEP 5. PICK
  Look up meaning x size in the table below for the video's set. A candidate must pass all of:
    a. its READ at sticker size matches the meaning (context_map "reads_at_sticker_size", "misread_as")
    b. its intensity equals the line's size (after Steps 2-3). Never one above.
    c. it reads at the planned width (context_map "min_width_px")
    d. its usable frames ("use_frames") can put the change 0.2 s after the trigger word ends, for the hold you need
    e. it is not already used in this video with another meaning
  Nothing passes -> stay in the listening face (lean or look toward the thing) or skip. Silence beats a wrong face.

STEP 6. FRAMES
  Clips with a build-up: start in the neutral part so the onset rides into the word:
    Side-Eye full 58-104 (left look) or 145-185 (right look); It's Over full from 1-12 (hands land ~24);
    No Way 15-62 (shout peak 37); Wail full 36+ (scream 38-62); Crack-Up 15+; Nah 80-141 (finger up 108+);
    Wince 1-30 for a smile only.
  Held clips (Grin, Jaw Drop, Wait What, Yikes, LOL, Sob, Stare, Ugh, Shook, Nervous, Hmm): swap in place from the listening
  face (2 f squash + 4 f settle).
  Listening loops: grey = sideeye_full 1-57 and 185-242; shirtless = stare 1-110. Never loop past stare frame 112.

OVER-REACTION RULES
  1. No Jaw Drop, Shook, No Way, It's Over or Wail on an even-money or "let's say" example ($100 at 0.50 -> $200 is the
     expected payout, not news), on a number the viewer can predict, or on the setup line before the number appears.
  2. No intensity-3 face on a rule or hypothetical unless it is the video's single climax AND the stimulus is on screen.
  3. Paper money, demo, testnet, "just an example" -> one level down.
  4. A routine fee, a normal score update, a definition -> the listening face.
  5. One intensity-3 per video; one It's Over OR one Wail, never both.
  6. Laughter (LOL, Crack-Up) only when the line is meant to be funny; never at a real loss, a scam's victims or a
     warning, and not as the close of a serious explainer.
  7. A static face (Ugh, Sob, Stare, Nervous) held over ~2 s starts to read as a photo: return to listening.

MISREAD RULES
  1. Faces that read as a SMILE at sticker size: Yikes, Wince (all of it), Nervous below ~400 px, Hmm, the Wail onset
     (wail_full 1-35, sticker 1-9), the No Way tail (66-75). Never on a loss, a cost, a risk to the viewer, a rug or a
     defeat: viewers read gloating.
  2. Faces that read as NEUTRAL: Stare, and Wait What below ~240 px. They carry listening or doubt, never a big reaction.
  3. Moving lips read as him SPEAKING: Nah (all of it), No Way 1-12, Wail full frame 1. Keep them off the voice-over's key
     word unless the gesture is the point.
  4. Side-Eye reads only in motion and only when the look points at the thing; place the sticker so it does.
  5. Jaw Drop has no valence of its own; Shook leans to fear (never for good news); Sob can read as disgust.
  6. It's Over reads from the hands: never crop them under a card.
  7. Check the planned width against "min_width_px". Below it, swap the clip; don't hope.

QUICK TEST BEFORE SHIPPING
  Say the line, then say the face's READ (not its name) as a reply: "Team loses, $0" -> "ha, nice" (Yikes) fails;
  -> "aw" (Sob flash) passes. "$200" -> "WHAT?!" (Jaw Drop) fails: the reply is bigger than the news.

SET GAPS (what mining the Cournot / Bagel recordings should look for)
  GREY: a rejection gesture (head shake / wag), disgust, a thinking or "clever" face, a calm risk face, more listening.
  SHIRTLESS: a clean closed-mouth win smile, a small sad loss (Sob-like), a side-look, a nod, a hands-on-head hero.
