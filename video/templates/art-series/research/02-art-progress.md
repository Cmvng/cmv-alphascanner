# Research 2: filming, editing and exporting art progress reels (22 Oct)

## Filming
- Shoot from a tripod and never move it between sessions; mark the tripod's floor spots. Point the camera straight at the drawing, using the 1x lens, offset a little to the side away from the drawing hand. [Parka Blogs 2020, Gurney 2022]
- Lock focus and exposure (AE/AF lock) on the paper; autofocus chases the hand.
  - Blackmagic Camera app (free) gives manual focus, locked white balance and shutter, 4K, and an interval time-lapse.
  - Suggested time-lapse: 4K 30 fps, one frame every 2 s. A 4-hour session gives about 4 minutes of footage.
- The phone's built-in time-lapse and Samsung's Hyperlapse pick their own intervals, so you get no control.
- Shutter 1/50 or 1/100 in 50 Hz countries, to avoid LED flicker.
- Light: room light off. One or two diffused daylight lamps at about 45°, the main one on the side away from the drawing hand.
- Film real-time close-ups with their natural sound: tools, hands, texture, blending, dust, blackened fingers. Mix them with the time-lapse. Slide or push the camera; don't zoom.
- Step out of frame for 5 s every 10–15 min, and take a clean progress shot at the end of every session.

## Editing
- Hook: show the payoff (the finished piece or the strongest current detail) in the first 1–3 s. TikTok: the message in the first 3 s, the hook by 6 s. Never open on titles or logos.
- Instagram ranks on watch time, likes and sends: cut idle moments, speed up repetitive stretches.
- Example: one artist condensed 18 hours into 40 s and got 962k views.
- Use straight cuts or dissolves, never gimmicky transitions. Build tension toward the reveal; before/after works.
- Reveal ideas: peel the tape off, pull back slowly, the artist standing beside the 62 × 94 cm piece for scale.
- Safe zones: keep text out of the top 14%, the bottom 35% and 6% at each side.

## Format
- Full-screen 9:16. Instagram said in 2021 that bordered Reels get recommended less, and artists report black bars cost views.
- Our square video on a black frame is a stopgap, not the target.
- Best: film landscape in 4K. A 9:16 crop from 4K is 1215 × 2160, sharper than 1080 × 1920.
- During the progress, pan slowly across the drawing. At the reveal, show the whole piece full-width with text above and below.

## Export and upload
- Export once, from the original files: 1080 × 1920, 30 fps, H.264, 10–20 Mb/s.
- Instagram: Settings → Data usage and media quality → "Upload at highest quality" ON, Data Saver OFF.
- TikTok: Upload HD ON.
- Never send footage through WhatsApp as a video (it tops out around 1280 × 718). Send it as a Document, or by cable or AirDrop.
- Delete CapCut's end clip before exporting. Instagram doesn't recommend Reels that carry another app's logo.

## Shot list for the next session
1. Tripod on its marks, 1x lens, level, straight on. Room light off, lamp at 45°. Airplane mode, charger in.
2. 5 s of the wall before starting.
3. Time-lapse: Blackmagic Camera, 4K 30 fps, 1 frame every 2 s, focus, white balance and shutter all locked.
4. Step out of frame for 5 s every 10–15 min.
5. Real-time close-ups of 5–10 s each, 15–20 cm from the paper, with sound.
6. One slow 10 s slide along the drawing.
7. A clean progress shot from the marks, with the artist out of frame.
8. Final day: the tape-peel reveal, a pull-back, and the artist beside the piece.
9. 30 s of room tone.
