## Reported (hollow type: outlet, date, anonymous source)
7. **The first report:** The New York Times, 29 Oct 2026. US intelligence believes Alice Huang, an executive linked to Megaspeed (Singapore), was passing information to Beijing.
   https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/29/us/politics/nvidia-china-jensen-huang-trump.html
   - The page is paywalled; BBC and AP (30 Oct) confirm what it said.
   - The alias "Huang Le" appears in BBC (30 Oct) and AFP (9 Nov), so it is left out of the video.
8. **The memo:** The New York Times, 6 Nov 2026. A US government memo said she was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security.
   https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/06/us/politics/chinese-spy-agency-nvidia-jensen-huang.html
9. **The romance claim:** Financial Times, 6 Nov 2026. The memo describes a romantic relationship. TechCrunch (7 Nov) says the NYT had the memo first and the FT followed with this claim.
   https://www.ft.com/content/a55a2a22-4479-4f93-a445-6147a5c6d790
10. **What is missing from the claims:**
    - The sources are "people briefed on the intelligence", and Reuters could not verify them.
    - No charges are reported.
    - Alice Huang did not respond (NYT, via BBC) or could not be reached (FT).
11. **China:** Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun, regular press conference, 30 Oct 2026, on the first report: "Relevant report is unsubstantiated. It is nothing but smears against China."
    https://www.mfa.gov.cn/eng/xw/fyrbt/lxjzh/202610/t20261030_12050434.html
12. **Megaspeed:**
    - NYT, 9 Oct 2025, "A Mystery CEO and Billions in Sales: Is China Buying Banned Nvidia Chips?": https://www.nytimes.com/2025/10/09/technology/nvidia-chips-china-megaspeed.html
