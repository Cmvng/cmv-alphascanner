Why was a post-quantum algorithm broken by a normal computer chip within an hour? And what does that mean for Starknet?
In July 2022, NIST, the US standards body, picked the first algorithms to protect against quantum computers. But four more were still being tested, including one called Sike.
Less than a month later, two researchers in Belgium broke Sike within an hour, on one core of a normal processor. No quantum computer involved.
So even maths built for the quantum age can fail, sometimes in ways nobody saw coming. Picking a good algorithm is only half the job. The other half is how fast you can switch when one breaks.
On Starknet, every account is a smart contract with its own signature check. So an account can switch to a different signature method, without a network-wide upgrade. And the proofs run on hash functions, instead of the elliptic curve maths that Shor's algorithm threatens.
As of the hub's 3 November 2026 update, a Falcon-512 account has been demonstrated on Starknet mainnet. And Falcon is one of the signature algorithms NIST picked in that same July 2022 announcement.
But it is a demo. Existing accounts do not change by themselves, and each owner chooses when to move. Starknet is preparing. It is not claiming to be quantum safe today.
The quantum hub shows what is live, and what still depends on Ethereum.
