Eight games. Eight wins. Twenty-four goals. This is how José Mourinho's Real Madrid became the team nobody can beat.

It's five straight wins in LaLiga, three in the Champions League, and the run is still alive.

The base is a 4-2-3-1. Aurélien Tchouaméni and Bernardo Silva sit side by side, so the back four is never exposed.

Without the ball, Madrid change shape to fit the opponent. In the Clásico, they dropped into a mid-to-low block, with the wingers tucked back to double up on Barcelona's wide men. Madrid had just 38% of the ball, and still created the better chances.

With the ball, it starts in the middle. Kylian Mbappé drops deep and drags a centre-back with him. Jude Bellingham arrives late into that space, and Marc Cucurella overlaps on the left.

Now watch the Clásico winner. Barcelona lose the ball. Bellingham turns, Arda Güler links it, Eduardo Camavinga breaks, and squares it to Mbappé. Empty net.

That's nine goals for Mbappé in the run, and he has scored in seven straight games.

When it gets tight, the bench decides it. Eight of the 24 goals came in the 80th minute or later. In Rome, Madrid were trailing 3-1, and three corners in seven minutes turned it into a 4-3 win.

The weak spot? Six goals conceded in three Champions League games.

Eight in a row. Can anyone stop them?
