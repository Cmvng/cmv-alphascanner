Seven wins to start the season. Then three straight defeats. No top-flight team had done that in 122 years. At Forest, City had 78% of the ball, and still lost three-one.

So what went wrong?

Forest sat in a 3-4-2-1 and gave City the ball. City made 753 passes. Forest made 206, and ran about six kilometres more.

Then Forest picked their moments. Six minutes in, City play out from the back. Jair Cunha steps out of the back three, tracks Joško Gvardiol, wins it high up the pitch, and scores.

On 18 minutes, a loose ball. Iliman Ndiaye and Elliot Anderson leave it to each other. Xaver Schlager gets there first, and Liam Delap runs from his own half, between the centre-backs. Two-nil.

Rayan Cherki's half-volley made it two-one. But City lost a duel on the edge of Forest's box, Morgan Gibbs-White and Delap ran the length of the pitch, and Delap finished it. Three-one.

Enzo Maresca on the duels and second balls: "We lost all of them."

That's the pattern. City conceded inside 11 minutes in all three defeats. They out-shot their opponents 48 to 26, but scored two and conceded six.

Erling Haaland hasn't scored in two games, and Antoine Semenyo, who played in all seven wins, is injured.

City aren't short of chances. They're losing the transitions. Can Maresca fix it?
